## Finding 1: Uber's AI Budget Crisis — The Enterprise Wake-Up Call

- 84% of Uber developers already using AI coding tools — not laggards

**Why this matters for Alizé:** Alizé's managed service model — where Alizé owns the outcome and manages consumption — looks increasingly attractive when the alternative is "burn budget and cap seats." ## Finding 2: Linux Foundation DNS-AID — Open Agent Discovery Takes Shape

**Source:** Le Monde Informatique / Linux Foundation, June 2, 2026

The Linux Foundation launched **DNS-AID** (Agent AI Discovery), an open-source project using standard DNS records as a decentralized agent discovery layer. Instead of registering agents with AWS Agent Catalog, Azure Agent Registry, or Google Cloud's equivalent, developers publish agent capabilities as DNS entries under `index._agents.{domain}`.

**Backers:** Cloudflare, Equinix, GoDaddy, Internet Systems Consortium, Deutsche Telekom, Amazon (contributions)
**Notably absent:** AWS — the hyperscaler pushing its own registry is sitting this one out

**Technical:** SDK Python + CLI, MCP (Model Context Protocol) compatible, no new infrastructure required, uses DNSSEC

**Implication for Alizé:** DNS-AID is still preliminary, but it signals a future where enterprise AI portfolios span multiple providers and models. When companies run agents from Mistral, Anthropic, Google, and open-source models simultaneously, they need a governance layer that isn't tied to any single cloud or model provider. This is the multi-agent, multi-provider world Alizé is being built for.

## Finding 3: Anthropic Opens Claude Mythos to ENISA — Proactive EU Compliance

**Source:** Bloomberg / Financial Times / GovInfoSecurity, June 1-2, 2026

Anthropic gave ENISA (EU cybersecurity agency) access to Claude Mythos — ahead of the August 2, 2026 deadline when Article 88 of the AI Act gives the European Commission formal supervisory powers over GPAI providers. This is a deliberate pre-compliance move.

**Context:** Before this, only the US (via AI Security Institute) and the UK had model access. Anthropic is building goodwill with European regulators before being legally required to cooperate.

**Why this matters:** Anthropic is signaling "we take EU compliance seriously." Alizé's positioning as the French compliance layer for companies using Claude (or any model) is reinforced when the model provider itself is making regulatory friends in Brussels.

## Finding 4: China Seals Training Data from EU AI Act Scrutiny

**Source:** Bloomberg / South China Morning Post / China IP Law Update, June 1-2, 2026

China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR Order No. 126, effective June 1, 2026) classifies AI training data, recommendation algorithms, and risk control models as administratively protected trade secrets. This directly conflicts with **EU AI Act Article 53**, which requires GPAI model providers to publish sufficiently detailed summaries of training data — including for open-weight models.

**Scale of the problem:** Qwen (Alibaba) hit 1 billion cumulative downloads on HuggingFace in January 2026, with 200,000 derivative models. Qwen family downloads reached 153.6 million in February 2026 alone — more than double all other major open-source models combined. **DeepSeek-R1** is equally widespread.

**The collision:** European companies that deployed Qwen or DeepSeek in production before June 2026 are now in an impossible position — Chinese law prohibits disclosure, EU law requires it. No resolution mechanism exists yet.

**Implication for Alizé:** Companies with Qwen or DeepSeek in their stack need legal/compliance guidance on this specific conflict. This is a qualified sales opportunity — "your Chinese model might be non-compliant through no fault of yours, here's how to assess and remediate."
