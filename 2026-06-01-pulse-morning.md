## Signal 2: EU AI Act — Enforcement Clock is Ticking (Deep-Dive)

Following up on the previous pulse's priority research direction: the **EU AI Act enters full enforcement on August 2, 2026** (12 months after official publication in the EU Official Journal, which was August 1, 2024).

### What's Enforcing When

| Date | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **August 2, 2026** | Prohibitions on unacceptable-risk AI apply (e.g., social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public) |
| **August 2, 2026** | High-risk AI systems must comply with requirements (transparency, data governance, accuracy, human oversight) |
| **August 2, 2027** | General-purpose AI (GPAI) model rules apply |
| **August 2, 2028** | Standards for high-risk areas (credit scoring, critical infrastructure) fully specified |

### The French SMB Problem

French PME/ETI (50-250 employees) face a specific compliance gap:
- Large enterprises (CAC40, big industry) have legal teams and compliance budgets
- Micro-enterprises (< 10 employees) are below the threshold for most high-risk AI obligations
- **The danger zone: mid-sized PME (50-250 employees) using AI in HR, finance, or operations** — they are subject to high-risk obligations but have no compliance infrastructure

This is Alizé's exact target segment. The question is: do these companies know they're in the high-risk zone?

### Key Compliance Requirements for High-Risk AI (What Alizé Should Monitor)

For AI systems in: employment decisions, credit scoring, critical infrastructure operations, law enforcement, border control, justice, democratic processes:

1. **Data governance:** Training data must be representative, free of discriminatory bias
2. **Technical documentation:** Every AI decision must be logged and explainable
3. **Human oversight:** A human must be able to review, override, and stop an AI decision
4. **Accuracy:** AI must meet published accuracy metrics
5. **Transparency:** Affected persons must be informed when interacting with AI

**Alizé's governance layer addresses requirements 2 and 3 directly.** The audit trail and human-in-the-loop oversight are literal compliance requirements under the EU AI Act high-risk framework.

### EU AI Act Enforcement Landscape in France

- **CNIL** (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés) is the primary enforcement authority for AI in France
- **apenFrance** (the French AI safety body) was created as part of the AI governance structure
- Fines: up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover for non-compliance
- **Current status (June 2026):** France is in the implementation phase. No major enforcement actions yet, but the clock is ticking toward August 2026.

### For Alizé

**EU AI Act compliance is a sales trigger, not a feature.** The pitch to a French PME using AI in their HR or finance workflow should be:
> "You're deploying AI in a high-risk domain. In 2 months, you're legally required to maintain audit trails and human oversight. We provide that as a managed service."

This converts a regulatory obligation into a purchase trigger. The Alizé brief should include an explicit EU AI Act compliance page — not as a legal disclaimer but as a value proposition.

## Signal 3: AI "Authority Problem" — Human Oversight is the Product

One more Maddyness op-ed worth integrating into Alizé's positioning: Calixte Descamps (Adequacy) argues that AI is rapidly shifting from "assistant" to "authority" — with users increasingly trusting AI decisions over human experts in medical, legal, and technical domains.

The key dynamic: **AI gives a single, confident answer. Humans give multiple sources to filter.** This asymmetry is powerful but dangerous.

### Why This is an Alizé Product Signal

Alizé's governance layer — audit trails, human-in-the-loop oversight, decision logs — is precisely what counters the "AI authority" problem. Every regulated industry (finance, healthcare, legal) is worried about:
1. AI making decisions it shouldn't
2. No paper trail when something goes wrong
3. No accountability when AI is wrong

Alizé's answer: **you always know what the AI did, you can always override it, you always have a log.**

This framing — "AI that works under your control, not AI that replaces your judgment" — is more credible than "compliance tool." It positions Alizé as the human-oversight infrastructure for AI that is becoming an authority.
