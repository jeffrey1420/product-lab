## Signal 1: Anthropic Mythos — AI Now Guarding Critical Infrastructure at Scale (MAJOR, June 2, 2026)

This is the most significant AI governance development since the EU AI Act entered enforcement.

### What Happened

Anthropic announced June 2, 2026 that it is expanding access to Mythos — their cybersecurity-focused model — to approximately 150 organizations across more than 15 countries. The program is called **Glasswing**.

Initial rollout (April 2026): ~50 partners, almost all US-based. Included CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Amazon, Google, Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft.

Results from initial phase: **10,000+ high/critical severity vulnerabilities found** in "the world's most sensitive programs."

New partners span: energy, water, healthcare, communications, and hardware sectors. Anthropic stated that a cyberattack on one of these partners **"could affect 100 million+ people."** French companies are reportedly among the new partners.

**ENISA (EU Agency for Cybersecurity) has been invited to join the program.** The European Commission confirmed this via AFP.

Initial restriction: In April, Anthropic had restricted Mythos distribution after it found thousands of vulnerabilities — the volume was itself alarming.

### The EU AI Act Implication — This Is Critical for Alizé

Critical infrastructure AI systems fall under **EU AI Act Annex III (high-risk AI systems)**. This means any French company using a Mythos-powered cybersecurity AI must:

- Conduct a **conformity assessment** before deployment
- Maintain an **Article 11 inventory** of all AI systems in use
- Implement **Article 14 human oversight** measures
- Generate and preserve **audit logs** (Article 12)
- Register the system in the **EU database** before going live (Article 51)

Mythos itself is operated by Anthropic, not the French company — but the **deployment context** (integrating Mythos into a French company's security stack) creates a compliance obligation for that company. This is exactly the governance surface Alizé was built for.

**The asymmetry:** Anthropic is a US company. French critical infrastructure companies are now sending their security operations through a US AI model. The CNIL and ANSSI may eventually have questions. Alizé could position as the **compliance bridge** for French companies deploying Mythos — documenting the integration, mapping it to EU AI Act requirements, and maintaining the audit trail.

### The Competitive Angle

Mythos is a pure cybersecurity agent. Vibe Work (Mistral) is a productivity/automation agent. These are two very different agent categories — but both are now being deployed into French enterprise stacks without EU AI Act compliance documentation.

**Alizé's opportunity:** Be the compliance layer for both. Vibe Work for operational workflows, Mythos for cybersecurity operations — both need Article 11 inventories, audit logs, and human oversight documentation when deployed in regulated French industries.

## Signal 3: Inria — Agentic AI Governance Gap Formally Documented (March 2026, now surfacing)

France's national research institute Inria published a thorough analysis of agentic AI in March 2026. Key points relevant to Alizé:

- Agentic AI enables "gérer mes mails cette semaine" vs "rédige un mail" — autonomy in action, not just information retrieval
- Key challenges: responsibility, transparency, consent, traceability, reproducibility
- "AI agentique transforme l'éthique de la recherche" — scientific publishing being automated without adequate governance
- "Les défis posés par l'IA agentique sont identiques à ceux de l'IA générative, mais amplifiés du fait de l'autonomie plus prononcée"
- **Critical insight:** agents integrate values and implicit objectives, which can amplify bias and misuse. Governance must be baked in.

**Alizé angle:** Inria is a respected French public institution. When French regulators (CNIL, ANSSI, DINR) look for guidance on agentic AI governance, Inria's framing will carry weight. Alizé's workflow-first positioning — emphasizing bounded, governed workflows over open-ended agents — aligns exactly with Inria's implied recommendation: build AI that stays within defined, auditable boundaries.
