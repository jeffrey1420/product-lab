## Deep-Dive: EU AI Act Annex III — Critical Infrastructure AI Compliance, Step by Step

The 06h pulse correctly identified that Mythos deployments in French critical infrastructure trigger EU AI Act Annex III obligations. This cycle drills into the specific compliance steps and maps Alizé services to each one.

### Which French Companies Are Affected

Annex III high-risk AI systems include:
- **Energy** — electricity/gas grid management, renewable energy optimization
- **Water** — drinking water distribution, wastewater treatment
- **Healthcare** — hospital diagnostic AI, medical imaging AI, clinical decision support
- **Communications** — telecom network management AI, cybersecurity AI
- **Transport** — traffic management, logistics optimization
- **Finance** — credit scoring (not Mythos-specific but relevant)

Mythos = AI-assisted cybersecurity for these sectors. A French energy company using Mythos to scan their operational technology networks is running a high-risk AI system.

### The 6-Step Compliance Pathway

**Step 1: Determine if the AI System Is High-Risk (Article 6)**

The company must assess whether their use of Mythos falls under Annex III. The key question: is the AI system making decisions or influencing decisions in a way that affects safety or fundamental rights?

For Mythos: yes — vulnerability scanning that informs patching decisions directly affects the security of critical infrastructure. This is high-risk.

**Alizé service:** High-Risk AI Classification Assessment — determine whether a specific AI deployment triggers Annex III.

---

**Step 2: Register the AI System in the EU Database (Article 51)**

Before going live, the company must register the AI system in the publicly managed EU database. This includes:
- The name and description of the AI system
- The name of the provider (Anthropic, or the French company if they wrap Mythos)
- The intended purpose and risk category
- Basic technical specifications

For Mythos: the complexity here is that Anthropic is the model provider and the French company is the deployer. The deployer has primary compliance responsibility under the EU AI Act unless Anthropic has pre-certified the system. Anthropic has not published EU AI Act conformity documentation.

**Alizé service:** EU AI Act Registration Package — prepare and file the registration dossier, handle the Anthropic/Mythos provider ambiguity.

---

**Step 3: Conduct a Conformity Assessment (Article 43)**

Before deployment, the company must assess whether the AI system meets the requirements of Annex I (technical standards) and Chapter III (requirements for high-risk AI). This is typically done via:
- Internal assessment (self-assessment for lower-risk Annex III systems)
- Notified body assessment (for highest-risk systems)

The assessment covers: data governance (Annex I), technical documentation, record-keeping, transparency, human oversight, accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity.

**Critical gap:** There is no publicly available conformity assessment for Anthropic Mythos. French companies deploying it cannot rely on vendor documentation — they must build their own, or purchase a pre-built compliance package.

**Alizé service:** Conformity Assessment Package — build the technical documentation, gap analysis against Annex I requirements, and the assessment report. This is the core Alizé offering.

---

**Step 4: Implement Human Oversight Measures (Article 14)**

The company must implement measures that allow humans to effectively oversee the AI system. For Mythos:
- Who reviews vulnerability reports before action is taken?
- What is the escalation path if Mythos flags a critical vulnerability at 3am?
- Who has authority to override Mythos recommendations?
- How are false positives (Mythos flags a non-vulnerability) handled?

The oversight measures must be documented and included in the user-facing documentation.

**Alizé service:** Human Oversight Framework — design the oversight workflow, define roles and escalation paths, document the oversight measures, and produce the Article 14 compliance annex.

---

**Step 5: Maintain the Audit Log (Article 12)**

All high-risk AI systems must maintain logs that are:
- Automatically recorded (not manually)
- Tamper-resistant
- Retained for a period appropriate to the system's risk level (minimum 6 months for most Annex III systems)
- Available to authorities on request

For Mythos: Anthropic likely maintains internal logs of model outputs. But the French company needs their own logs of how Mythos outputs were used — what decisions were made based on Mythos findings, who reviewed them, what actions were taken.

**Alizé service:** Audit Log Architecture — design the log capture layer between Mythos outputs and the company's ticketing/patching system, implement the retention policy, and ensure CNIL/ANSSI audit readiness.

---

**Step 6: Ongoing Monitoring and Reporting (Articles 9 and 72)**

Post-deployment obligations:
- Continuously monitor the AI system's performance and compliance
- Report serious incidents (Article 72) to the relevant market surveillance authority
- Update the EU database registration if significant changes are made
- Re-assess conformity if the system's purpose or risk level changes

For Mythos: if Mythos updates its model (which Anthropic does frequently), does that constitute a "significant change" requiring re-registration? The answer is likely yes for critical infrastructure deployments.

**Alizé service:** Ongoing Compliance Monitoring — quarterly compliance reviews, incident report drafting, model update assessment, annual EU AI Act health check.
