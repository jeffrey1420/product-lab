## New Signal 1: Google Formalizes the Agent Platform — Full Stack from Chip to Inbox

**Source:** Google Cloud Next '26 (April 2026, confirmed in ongoing coverage), The Next Web

Google's Cloud Next 2026 was explicitly framed as "The Agentic Cloud" by Thomas Kurian. The key consolidation moves:

**Platform rebranding:**
- Vertex AI → **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform** — the canonical enterprise agent platform from Google
- Google Agentspace absorbed into unified Gemini Enterprise product
- 200+ models in Model Garden including Anthropic Claude (Opus, Sonnet, Haiku)

**New products:**
- **Workspace Studio**: No-code agent builder for business users — describe automations in plain language ("every Friday ping me to update my tracker"), connects to Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive, Meet, Chat, plus third-party: Asana, Jira, Mailchimp, Salesforce
- **Agent Garden**: Prebuilt agent solutions for customer service, data analysis, creative tasks (partner agents from Box, Workday, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Dun & Bradstreet, S&P Global)
- **Project Mariner**: Web-browsing agent (Gemini 2.0), 83.5% on WebVoyager benchmark, 10 concurrent cloud VM tasks
- **Managed MCP servers**: Apigee as API-to-agent bridge — standardized agent-to-tool communication
- **Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol**: Now in production at **150 organizations** (not pilot) — agents built on different platforms routing real tasks between each other. Governed by the Linux Foundation

**Infrastructure:**
- TPU 8t (training) and TPU 8i (inference) — 8th gen TPUs, up to 121 ExaFlops per superpod
- Virgo Network: AI data center fabric linking up to 134,000 TPU chips at 47 petabits/sec bi-sectional bandwidth

**Why this matters for Alizé:**

This is a double-edged signal. On one hand, Google is validating the enterprise agent market as the key battleground. On the other, the full-stack play (chip → model → platform → workspace → agents) means enterprises will face strong pressure to go all-in on Google, which reduces the need for independent governance layers.

However, Google's full-stack ownership is exactly the dynamic that creates compliance and vendor-lock-in concerns — the same concerns Alizé is positioned to address. Enterprises adopting Google's agent platform will still need independent oversight, audit trails, and governance guardrails. Alizé can position as the **independent governance layer on top of any platform** — not competing with Google, but ensuring enterprises can deploy Google agents (or Mistral, or Anthropic) with proper controls.

The A2A protocol in production at 150 orgs is the most directly Alizé-relevant signal: as agents proliferate and communicate across platforms, the need for governance, tracing, and audit grows exponentially. Alizé's workflow governance positioning is well-aligned.

**Takeaway:** A2A in production (150 orgs) is a leading indicator that multi-agent deployments are real and growing. Governance tooling is becoming necessary infrastructure, not theoretical.

## Structural Trend: Platform Wars Heating Up, Governance Gap Widening

The three signals together paint a clear picture:

1. **Google** is going full-stack (chip to workspace agent) and betting vertical integration beats best-of-breed
2. **OpenAI** is going enterprise-first via channel partners (Cognizant, CGI) and hitting 40% enterprise revenue
3. **Anthropic** is building MCP ecosystem scale and governance narrative (pause paper)

All three are racing to own the agent platform. None of them are focused on what happens when enterprises actually deploy agents in regulated industries with audit requirements, cross-border data constraints, and internal governance policies.

**This is Alizé's white space.** The platform players compete for ownership. Alizé provides the governance layer that makes any platform deployment auditable, constrained, and compliant.
