## Research Deep-Dive: Context Engineering as Product Concept

Given the quiet window, conducted a focused research session on an emerging theme from recent readings: **context engineering** as the missing layer in enterprise AI agent deployment.

**Source material:** Trace AI $3M seed round coverage (TechCrunch, Feb 2026); Automation Anywhere Context Intelligence Graph announcement.

### The Theme: "2024 was prompt engineering. 2025 was agentic AI. 2026 is context engineering."

The observation from multiple recent signals: enterprises deploying AI agents are hitting a wall not because the models are incapable, but because the agents don't have the right context to act reliably in complex organizational environments.

**Trace's formulation (February 2026):**
> "OpenAI and Anthropic are building these brilliant interns that can be leveraged within the company. We're building the manager that knows where to put them."

Trace's approach: build a knowledge graph from a company's existing tools (email, Slack, Airtable, ERPs) so that when an agent is invoked, it receives exactly the context needed for its specific sub-task — not all context, and not irrelevant context.

**Automation Anywhere's formulation (May 2026):**
> "In complex workflows, more context is not always better. Irrelevant information can slow execution, increase costs, expose sensitive data unnecessarily, and introduce inaccurate or inconsistent decisions."
> — Context Intelligence Graph, part of their Process Reasoning Engine

Both are independently arriving at the same insight: **context selection is the hard problem in enterprise AI agent deployment.**

### Why This Matters for Alizé

Alizé's positioning is "AI workflow governance." The governance layer sits above the routing layer. But context engineering — the problem Trace is solving — is adjacent to and potentially upstream of Alizé's governance concern.

**Three possible positions for Alizé in the context stack:**

1. **Alizé = context audit trail** — When an agent selects context (pulls from email, CRM, ERP), Alizé logs what was pulled, when, by which agent, and whether it was authorized under the enterprise's EU AI Act obligations. This is Alizé as the "context provenance" layer.

2. **Alizé = context guardrail** — Alizé enforces what context an agent *can* access, based on the agent's defined purpose and the data classification of the enterprise. This prevents agents from "over-reaching" into sensitive data they don't need for their task. Directly addresses the EU AI Act Article 11 (data governance) requirement: "AI systems shall be designed and developed to minimize the use of data to what is necessary."

3. **Alizé = context compliance scorer** — For each workflow, Alizé produces a "context appropriateness" score: how well did the agent's context selection match what was minimally necessary? This is an audit and improvement tool for enterprise AI governance.

**The insight:** The EU AI Act Articles 11 and 12 effectively mandate context engineering for high-risk AI systems. Article 11 requires data governance measures that "ensure that the training, validation and testing data is relevant, sufficiently representative, free of errors and complete." Article 12 requires technical robustness to prevent misuse of data. An agent that blindly pulls all available context is non-compliant by design.

**Product direction implication:** Alizé could position as "context governance for enterprise AI agents" — enforcing minimal context use, logging context access, and producing compliance documentation for EU AI Act Articles 11–12. This directly addresses the gap between agent platforms (which route tasks) and governance requirements (which constrain how agents access data).

**Trace comparison:** Trace builds context *for* agents. Alizé governs context *of* agents. These are complementary, not competitive. Alizé could integrate with Trace's knowledge graph as a governance layer on top.
