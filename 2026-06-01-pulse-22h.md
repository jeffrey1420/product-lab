## Signal 2: LangGraph Enterprise Adoption — Graph-Based Agent Orchestration Goes Mainstream

LangGraph (MIT-licensed, from LangChain) has published a refreshed product page with concrete enterprise reference customers: **Klarna, Replit, Elastic, and Ally** (the financial institution).

### What LangGraph Provides

LangGraph is positioned explicitly as an **agent runtime and orchestration framework** — not just a development library. Key enterprise features:

- **Human-in-the-loop interrupts** — ability to pause agent execution and require human approval before continuing
- **Multi-agent architectures** — hierarchical control flows, not just single-agent loops
- **Stateful memory** — conversation histories persisting across sessions
- **Agent tracing** — full visibility into agent reasoning chains
- **Streaming-first** — token-by-token output for real-time UX

LangGraph is MIT-licensed open-source; the commercial product is **LangSmith** (observability, debugging, deployment). This is the same model as CrewAI: open core + paid observability/enterprise layer.

### Why This Matters for Alizé

LangGraph's enterprise momentum confirms that **graph-based agent orchestration** is becoming the standard architecture for serious production deployments. When Alizé designs supervisor agent frameworks, they should:

1. Be LangGraph-compatible or document how they integrate with LangGraph-based stacks
2. Position as the governance/compliance layer *on top of* LangGraph orchestration
3. Reference LangGraph's human-in-the-loop interrupt feature as baseline capability — Alizé's supervisor framework is the production-grade version of this for regulated industries

LangGraph's Ally reference (financial institution) is the same target vertical as Alizé's sweet spot: finance, insurance, regulated industries.

## Signal 3: Inria Frames Agentic AI for French Enterprise — Institutional Validation

France's national research institute (Inria) published a major explainer on agentic AI in March 2026 (updated since), providing institutional framing that will influence how French enterprises understand the technology.

### Key Inria Framing

- Agentic AI = systems that pursue high-level goals autonomously, plan actions, use external tools — **distinguished from** simple LLM or chatbot
- The functional shift: from "draft this email" → "manage my emails this week"
- **Not a scientific rupture** but a functional and systemic one — this is important framing because it makes the technology feel incremental and manageable for enterprises
- Applications identified: project management assistants, autonomous cybersecurity agents, financial agents, advanced enterprise automation
- Research impact: AI Scientist v2 (April 2026) can execute full research pipelines autonomously — scientific publishing is already affected

### The French Terminology Choice

Inria and the French AI Council deliberately prefer **"IA agentique"** over **"agents IA"**. Rationale: avoids the binary framing of automation vs. human control. Agentic systems **include** human validation checkpoints — the term itself signals that this is not about replacing humans.

This framing aligns perfectly with Alizé's positioning: **agentic AI needs supervision, and Alizé provides the supervision framework**. The institutional vocabulary in France is already shifting toward the narrative Alizé needs.

### Inria's Research Priorities in Agentic AI

- Game theory, distributed systems, LLM orchestration
- Standardization of agent communication protocols
- Human-robot interaction and psychological/sociological dimensions
- **Evaluation and security of AI agents** — directly relevant to Alizé's governance thesis
- Applications: autonomous vehicles, software development, healthcare, Industry 4.0/5.0
