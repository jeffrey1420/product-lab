## Signal 1: EU AI Act — Omnibus VII Update (May 7, 2026 Political Agreement)

The EU Council and Parliament reached a provisional political agreement on amending the EU AI Act as part of the broader Omnibus VII simplification package. This is the most significant change to the Act's timeline since it entered into force in August 2024.

### Updated Timeline

| Milestone | Original Date | New Date | Status |
|-----------|--------------|----------|--------|
| Prohibited AI practices (social scoring, manipulation, facial scraping) | Feb 2, 2025 | Feb 2, 2025 | **LIVE** |
| General-purpose AI model rules (transparency, copyright, safety) | Aug 2, 2025 | Aug 2, 2025 | **LIVE** |
| Transparency obligations (chatbots, deepfakes, AI content watermarking) | Aug 2, 2026 | Dec 2, 2026 (grace period cut to 4 months) | Pending formal adoption |
| High-risk standalone AI (Annex III: HR screening, credit scoring, biometric ID, etc.) | Aug 2, 2026 | **Dec 2, 2027** | Pending formal adoption |
| High-risk AI embedded in regulated products (medical devices, machinery) | Aug 2, 2027 | Aug 2, 2028 | Pending formal adoption |
| National AI regulatory sandboxes (one per member state) | Aug 2, 2026 | Aug 2, 2027 | Extended |

### Critical: The Standards Gap

**As of May 2026, no harmonized EU AI Act standards have been formally published.** Zero. CEN-CELLEC JTC 21 is developing them — the first draft standard (prEN 18286 for AI quality management systems) went through public comment in late 2025, but no published, Official Journal-referenced standard exists.

The implication: companies preparing for high-risk AI compliance **cannot** point to a harmonized standard and say "we followed this." They're working directly from the Articles, referencing international standards (ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894), and using Article 41 common specifications as a fallback.

**This creates a significant window for governance tooling.** Organizations that start compliance work now shape their approach before standards lock in. Alizé's governance layer is precisely the kind of infrastructure companies need during this standards vacuum.

### New Prohibition (Unexpected)

The Omnibus VII agreement adds a new category of prohibited AI practices: systems generating non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM). This prohibition takes effect **December 2, 2026** — faster than the high-risk AI compliance deadline.

### SME Relief

The Omnibus extends regulatory exemptions previously limited to SMEs (under 250 employees) to small mid-cap companies (SMCs) — threshold being negotiated around **up to 500 employees**. This directly benefits Alizé's target segment (50-250 employee PME/ETI).

## Signal 2: French SME AI Adoption — The Readiness Gap

The most concrete French SME AI adoption data comes from a **Qonto study (October 2025, 1,600 European SME leaders surveyed via Appinio)**:

### Headline Numbers

- **46% of European SMEs use AI tools like ChatGPT daily**
- Only **24% have digital accounting systems**
- Only a fraction of the 46% using AI tools have deployed them in a structured, governed way

The gap between AI tool usage and basic digital infrastructure is the defining characteristic of the European SME AI landscape in 2025-2026.

### France-Specific Context

- France has **more than 750 AI-focused companies** (Atera, 2026)
- AI accounts for approximately **27% of all French venture capital deployed** (Knowlee, May 2026)
- Funding for **agentic AI specifically rose 65%+ in 2025** vs 2024
- France is building at the foundation layer (models, agent platforms, RLOps) — not just application layer

This creates a specific dynamic: French SMEs are adopting AI tools (driven by consumer-grade ChatGPT/Claude) faster than they're building the internal infrastructure to govern those tools. The market opportunity for Alizé is serving this gap.

- France leads the EU with the most agentic AI startups per capita (Paris: Mistral AI, H Company, Poolside, Dust, AMI Labs, 2501.ai)
- France's sovereign AI strategy (S3 program, ChooseAI initiative, CNRS compute partnerships) is actively funding the infrastructure layer

### For Alizé

The 46% AI tool usage rate represents **massive latent demand for governance**. Those 46% are using ChatGPT for business tasks — many with no formal policy, no audit trail, no compliance framework. Alizé's target customer is:

1. The 46% who are already using AI tools and need to bring structure
