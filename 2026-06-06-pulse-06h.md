## H1 2026 Mid-Year Audit: Six Things That Actually Changed

The last 6 months have reshaped enterprise AI in ways the January 2026 trend pieces missed. Three data points from Apptad's mid-year audit (June 2026) are required reading for anyone building in this space.

---

## #1 — MCP Is the Integration Standard. It's Not Close.

In November 2024, Anthropic published a technical spec for how AI systems connect to tools and data. Nobody thought it would matter at scale. Eighteen months later, it is the closest thing the enterprise AI economy has to a universal standard.

**The numbers:**
- MCP TypeScript + Python SDKs: **97 million monthly downloads** as of March 2026, up from ~2M at launch — a 4,750% growth rate
- Public MCP server registry: **9,400+ entries**; private enterprise servers estimated at 3-4x that
- Stacklok 2026 report: **41% of surveyed software orgs** are in limited or broad production with MCP servers

**What sealed it:** Anthropic donated MCP to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in December 2025 (co-founded with Block and OpenAI). It stopped being a vendor protocol and became infrastructure. ChatGPT, Cursor, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, VS Code, and the major cloud providers now consume MCP natively. Salesforce, Snowflake, MDM platforms (Reltio AgentFlow, Informatica CLAIRE Agents, STIBO), and the major cloud providers have all exposed their capabilities as MCP endpoints.

**The procurement consequence:** "Does your platform expose its capabilities via MCP?" is now a standard enterprise buying question — alongside "Does it run in our cloud?" Vendors that say no are on the defensive. Vendors that say yes are getting slotted into architectures nobody had on the diagram a year ago.

**Strategic implication for Alizé:** MCP compatibility is becoming a soft gate in enterprise procurement. Alizé's managed service layer should position as an **MCP orchestration hub** — connecting enterprise's fragmented tool landscape (ERP, CRM, ITSM, legacy systems) as MCP endpoints that Mistral Vibe or any other agent can consume. This reframes integration complexity as a managed service feature, not a DIY problem.

## #2 — AI Agents Are in Production, But the Shakeout Is Starting

Gartner's mid-year data: **31% of enterprises now have at least one AI agent in production.** Up from effectively zero two years ago. The category is mainstream — but mainstream is uneven, and the unevenness is the profitable part.

**Production rates by industry:**
| Industry | % with agent in production |
|---|---|
| Telecom | 48% |
| Retail / CPG | 47% |
| Banking / Insurance | 47% |
| Software / Tech | 42% |
| Manufacturing | 30% |
| Healthcare | 21% |
| Public Sector | 18% |

**The leaders pulled ahead by doing the unsexy work first** — data, identity, and governance layers underneath the agent. The trailing industries (healthcare, public sector) aren't slow or unsophisticated. They're harder to defend regulatorily, and the data foundation work costs more in their estates.

**The cautionary data:** Gartner predicts **40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027** due to runaway costs, unclear ROI, and governance failures. Only 25% of AI initiatives currently deliver expected ROI. Only 16% reach enterprise-wide scale.

**This is the managed service opportunity, stated plainly:** Enterprises are discovering that building an AI agent and running one in production are different businesses. The 40% cancellation rate is not a market problem — it's a market signal. Alizé's offer ("we design, deploy, and operate your AI workflows") directly addresses the reason projects fail: operationalization complexity, not model capability.

## #3 — AI FinOps Became a Discipline Overnight

Two years ago, AI cost management was an edge concern. Now it's universal: **98% of FinOps practices manage AI spend** — up from 63% in 2025 and 31% in 2024.

**Enterprise AI budget composition (H1 2026):**
| Category | % of AI budget |
|---|---|
| Foundation model API spend | 28% |
| GPU / compute (training + inference) | 22% |
| **Data foundation (MDM, governance, identity)** | **16%** |
| Integration / MCP / agent platform | 11% |
| Evaluation, observability, AI FinOps tooling | 9% |
| Vector stores / retrieval infrastructure | 8% |
| Human-in-loop review | 6% |

**The surprising structural line:** Data foundation (MDM, governance, identity resolution) at 16% is now the third-largest budget line — a boardroom-level structural change. Human-in-loop review, dismissed as a temporary cost, has become a **structural cost line** at 6% that the coming agent shakeout will likely expand.

**Note:** SAP paid a reported multi-billion dollars in March 2026 to acquire a master data company. This is the market confirming the same data fragmentation problem that Alizé's workflow readiness score is designed to diagnose.

**FinOps implication for Alizé:** Alizé's managed service pricing model should explicitly include cost governance. Enterprises want predictability — fixed monthly fees with transparent cost accounting. The shift from "AI project" to "AI workflow operation" is the commercial wedge.

## #4 — EU AI Act: August 2, 2026 Is Real, But High-Risk Deadlines Slid

The EU Omnibus political agreement (May 6, 2026) pushed the high-risk system deadlines significantly:
- Annex III systems (recruitment, credit, law enforcement, education, border): **August 2, 2026 → December 2, 2027**
- Annex I embedded systems (medical devices, machinery, vehicles): **August 2, 2027 → August 2, 2028**

**What did NOT change — still August 2, 2026:**
- **Transparency obligations (Article 50):** must disclose when users interact with an AI system
- **Watermarking:** grace period expires December 2, 2026

**Implication:** The "August 2026 compliance fire drill" framing from Alizé's earlier positioning is now out of date. High-risk obligations are 17-29 months away. The EU AI Act is now a **planning horizon, not a scramble**. Alizé's pitch should drop urgency language and lead with the managed service value proposition: "We build and run your AI workflows — compliant and operational when the deadline arrives."

## Synthesis: Three Product Angles Worth Building On

### Angle 1 — MCP Integration Hub
Every enterprise has fragmented tools. Every agent needs integration. MCP is the standard. Alizé as the managed service that handles enterprise MCP endpoint setup, maintenance, and operation — connecting legacy ERP/CRM/ITSM to agentic workflows — is a concrete, differentiated offer. Not "we build AI," but "we connect your systems to AI."

### Angle 2 — The Managed Service That Replaces the Failed DIY Agent
40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027. The reason: operationalization complexity. Alizé should position explicitly as the "operationalization partner" for enterprises whose internal or SI-led projects failed or are failing. This is uncomfortable but defensible — and there's a real market of frustrated AI project owners.

### Angle 3 — Sovereign Stack for French PME/ETI
Mistral Vibe (French model) + French data residency + Alizé managed service = complete French enterprise AI stack. No US hyperscaler dependency. No GDPR exposure from US data flows. For mid-market French companies, this is a credible and increasingly attractive proposition as US-EU data transfer legal risk (Schrems II) persists.
